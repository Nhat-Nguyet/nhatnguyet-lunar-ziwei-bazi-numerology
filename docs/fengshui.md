# Feng shui: methods, sources and where the schools disagree

`@nhatnguyet/fengshui` (TypeScript) and `nhatnguyet.fengshui` (Python) port
the feng shui functions of the Nhat Nguyet reference engine (the Go engine
behind nhatnguyet.org). Every answer is
checked against `ca-kiem/fengshui.json`: 3,083 vectors generated by that
engine, passed field by field by both ports.

These are the rules of traditional schools as their books state them. They
describe what a school says about a direction or a measurement; they are not
verified effects, and nothing here predicts what will happen in a house.

## Eight Mansions (Bát Trạch, 八宅)

**Life gua.** By the 180-year cycle of the three eras in the *Xieji Bianfang
Shu* (協紀辨方書, section on the nine palaces of men and women): the Jia-Zi
year 1864 opens the upper era; a male starts at palace 1 and moves back one
palace a year, a female starts at 5 and moves forward. A result of 5, which
has no trigram, becomes 2 (Kun) for a male and 8 (Gen) for a female. The
shortcut on the last two digits of the year gives the same numbers only for
1900 to 2099; the engine does not use it.

**Year boundary.** The engine takes the lunar year of the birth date, the
year turning at the lunar New Year (Tết) in Vietnam time (UTC+7). Many
Chinese-language books turn the year at the Start of Spring (立春, about
4 February) instead. The two rules disagree for anyone born between the two
dates (Tết falls between 21 January and 20 February). The engine has no
second rule, so the library offers no `schools` option; to follow a book that
uses the Start of Spring, pass the year number: `lifeGua(1989, "male")`.

**The eight stars.** The direction of each star for each trigram follows the
*Bazhai Mingjing* (八宅明鏡) and its verse on the great wandering stars; the
engine checks the table by flipping lines of the trigrams and by symmetry.
The order is the engine's: four favourable stars from Sheng Qi to Fu Wei,
four unfavourable from Huo Hai to Jue Ming. Books do not rank them the same
way: the *Rudi Yan Quanshu* (入地眼全書) names only three good stars (Sheng
Qi, Tian Yi, Yan Nian, not Fu Wei), calls Jue Ming the worst and Wu Gui the
next, and puts Huo Hai level with Liu Sha. `favorable` is the four-and-four
split; read the order as the engine's, not as a scale.

**House gua.** A house takes the trigram of its sitting side (the back), as
the engine's wealth-corner function reads it. East group houses sit in Kan,
Zhen, Xun or Li; west group houses in Qian, Kun, Gen or Dui.

Bát Trạch is one school among several. Flying Star and landform (luantou)
schools reach different conclusions about the same house.

## The 24 mountains (二十四山)

24 = 8 stems (the ten stems without Wu 戊 and Ji 己, which belong to the
center) + 12 branches + 4 corner trigrams (Qian, Kun, Gen, Xun). Each sector
is 15 degrees; Zi is centered on north, so it runs 352.5 to 7.5. A sector
includes its lower edge: 7.5 is Gui. Each 45-degree direction holds three
mountains, the Earth, Heaven and Human dragons of the Three Cycles (三元龍),
the middle one carrying the direction's own name.

**Yin and yang, element.** The engine deliberately does not give a
school-neutral yin or yang per mountain: schools assign it differently.
`xuanKongPolarity` is the assignment the Flying Star school (Shen) uses to
decide whether a star flies forward or backward, and is labelled as such.
The engine gives no element per mountain either (the "proper", "double
mountain" and "Hong Fan" element systems disagree); `trigramElement` is the
element of the trigram that governs the direction.

## Xuan Kong flying stars (玄空飛星)

**Chart.** Rules of the Shen school (沈氏玄空): the period star enters the
center and flies forward along the Lo Shu path (center, Qian, Dui, Gen, Li,
Kan, Kun, Zhen, Xun). The base star in the sitting palace enters the center
as the mountain star, the one in the facing palace as the facing star. They
fly forward when the mountain of the same dragon in the trigram of that
number is yang, backward when yin; a 5 takes the polarity of the sitting (or
facing) mountain itself.

**Patterns.** The four the engine names: prosperous sitting and facing (旺山旺向),
up the mountain down the water (上山下水), double stars at facing (双星到向),
double stars at sitting (双星到坐). Other charts have `pattern: null`.

**Not computed.** Replacement stars (替卦, used by many books when the facing
is within about 3 degrees of a mountain edge) give a different chart; the
engine computes only the plain facing, and the result says
`replacementStars: false`. Monthly stars are not in the engine either.

**Roles of the stars (`starQi`).** The common split: the period star is
prosperous, the next two growing, the previous one retreating, the three
before that killing, the other two dead. The killing level follows Hồ Kinh
Quốc, *Tìm hiểu cổ dịch Huyền Không học*. Variants: some books count only the
next star as growing (the second is "advancing"), some split off a declining
star, and many call Five Yellow killing in every period but 5.

**Castle gates (城門).** The two palaces beside the facing palace; the one
whose number pairs with the facing palace in the He Tu (1 and 6, 2 and 7,
3 and 8, 4 and 9) is the primary gate. Its base star enters the center and
flies by the polarity of the mountain of the facing's dragon; the gate is
usable when the period star lands back on it. Some schools accept only the
primary gate, or test gates from the sitting side.

**Periods.** Twenty years each, nine to the 180-year cycle, counted from the
Start of Spring: period 9 runs 2024 to 2043. This is the even split of the
Flying Star school; the Xuan Kong Da Gua school uses periods of uneven
length. Period 5 has no palace of its own: books send its first ten years to
Kun and the last ten to Gen, or all twenty to both, so the library gives no
Zheng Shen and Ling Shen palace for it.

**Annual stars.** The center star falls by one each year (2026 One White,
2027 Nine Purple) and always flies forward. The year counts from the Start of
Spring: for a date in January or early February use the previous year.

## Luban rulers (魯班尺)

Three rulers, each divided into equal sectors; a length is reduced modulo
the ruler and read in the sector it falls in. A length exactly on an edge
belongs to the upper sector. The engine works in tenths of a millimetre
(522 / 8 is 65.25 mm, not the 65 mm many tables print).

| Ruler | Length | Used for | Sectors |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clear-opening` | 52.2 cm | clear openings: doors, windows, vents | 8 (Gui Ren, Xian Huo, Tian Zai, Tian Cai, Fu Lu, Gu Du, Tian Zei, Zai Xiang), 5 sub-sectors each |
| `luban` | 42.9 cm | solid pieces: plinths, beds, cabinets | 8 (Cai, Bing, Li, Yi, Guan, Jie, Hai, Ben), 4 sub-sectors each |
| `dinglan` | 38.8 cm | altars, ritual objects, graves | 10 (Ding, Hai, Wang, Ku, Yi, Guan, Si, Xing, Shi, Cai), no sub-sectors |

Sources and disagreements, as noted in the engine: the eight-character ruler
財病離義官劫害本 is the one the *Luban Jing* (魯班經) describes, the Men Guang
ruler, whose length the book gives as 1 chi 4 cun 4 fen of the carpenter's
foot (45.4 cm in the Vietnamese translation by Ngọ Vinh); 42.9 cm is the
length in common use in Vietnam. The 52.2 cm and 38.8 cm rulers are not in
the book; they are the rulers in common use. Vietnamese sources disagree on
sectors 6 and 8 of the 42.9 cm ruler (Nạn and Mạng against Kiếp and Bản); the
engine follows the characters engraved on the rulers. Sector 8 has a variant:
the translation of the *Luban Jing* reads 吉 and notes that some books changed
it to 本. Sources agree on the ten sectors of the 38.8 cm ruler but not on
their sub-sectors, so the engine leaves them out.

A sub-sector carries `pinyin`, `nameEn`, `nameVi` and `nameZh`. Its `name`
follows `terms` like every other name (pinyin by default in English, the
Chinese characters with `terms: "han"`, Vietnamese with `terms: "vi"`).
`nameEn` is a short literal translation of the two characters, in the same
spirit as the sector meanings: 口舌 "Quarrels", 富贵 "Wealth and rank", 进宝
"Treasure gained". The names in Chinese are simplified characters, like every
other name of the library; only the sector's `han` keeps the traditional
characters engraved on the rulers (福祿), and the command line prints the
simplified form (`nameZh`) so one screen uses one script.

## Names

Every function takes `lang` ("en", "vi", "zh") and, for English, `terms`
("pinyin", "han", "vi") for names without an established English translation:
mountains, trigrams, the eight stars, the Luban sectors and sub-sectors. Directions, the nine
stars ("One White", 一白, Nhất Bạch), elements, eras and pattern names are
translated whatever `terms` says. Each object also carries `pinyin`, `han`
and `nameVi`. Star `gloss` is a literal translation of the two characters,
not a reading.
